## 2. What Did I Achieve? (My New Capabilities)

Looking back at Day 1, the transformation in my technical and analytical capability is massive. Here is what I can confidently do now that I completely couldn't do a month ago:

* **Structure Chaotic Business Problems:** Using the **5W2H framework** (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How, How Much), I can dissect a vague issue and turn it into a clear, actionable problem statement. 
* **Build Air-Tight Logic Traps:** I learned how to use **MECE** and **logic trees** to break down large, ambiguous challenges into smaller, bite-sized components without overlapping or missing critical details.
* **Execute the EGAD Framework:** I can seamlessly map out a project from start to finish—**Explaining** the business problem, **Gathering** the data requirements, **Analysing** the patterns, and **Deploying** data-driven recommendations using Agile sprints.
* **Wield Generative AI Responsibly:** Instead of typing basic questions into LLMs, I now apply **structured prompt engineering** (assigning roles, giving explicit context, defining output constraints) to generate code snippets, draft text, and refine algorithms.
* **Think Like a Programmer:** I can design logical solutions to problems using **flowcharts** and write clean **pseudocode** incorporating control flows (like `if-else` statements and boolean operators) before touching actual language syntax.

## 3. What Didn't Go Well, and What Did I Learn From It?

No learning curve is completely smooth. Two main areas stretched me to my limits this month:

### Developing the "Why" and "What" Mindset
At the start of the course, I tended to take data at face value. If sales dropped, I would note *what* happened, but I struggled to build the muscle to dig deep into *why* it happened. During the practical exercises, I kept getting pulled back to superficial observations. 
* **The Lesson:** I learned that data without context is just noise. I started forcing myself to look at variables holistically—asking what external and internal factors could drive a trend, rather than just reporting the numbers.

### Communication as a Data Analyst
Translating technical findings into business-friendly language was surprisingly tough. Our exercises required us to draft public posts and summaries explaining different analytical scenarios to stakeholders. My first few attempts were either way too technical or way too vague.
* **The Lesson:** I began to find my footing by realizing that non-technical stakeholders care about outcomes, risks, and next steps—not the complexity of the backend logic. While I am not completely where I want to be yet, these posting exercises helped me start building a solid foundation in data storytelling.

## 4. Breakthrough Moments I Want to Remember

Two distinct milestones from this course completely changed my perspective on data analytics:

### Breakthrough 1: The Sales Drop Scenario
This exercise was a major turning point for me. When looking at a sudden decrease in revenue, my immediate instinct was to search for a single, direct cause. The case study forced us to stop looking at just the *what* (the revenue dip) and start investigating the *why*. It clicked for me that a single metric is often the product of interlocking factors—like a simultaneous change in marketing spend and competitor pricing. It taught me to always look for the story behind the trend.

### Breakthrough 2: The StreamSphere Project
The StreamSphere scenario was where everything we learned crystallized into a unified workflow. We had to resolve a classic corporate deadlock: *Is low platform engagement due to a lack of user awareness (Marketing's view) or a lack of relevant content (Product's view)?*

> By deploying the **EGAD framework** alongside an **Agile approach**, I learned how to treat these opposing views as competing hypotheses that can be tested with real data. 

We used a logic tree to split the problem into measurable KPIs (click-through rates vs. watch-time retention). This exercise made me realize that data isn't just about generating pretty charts; it is the ultimate objective mediator in business strategy.
